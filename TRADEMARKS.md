## What you may do

Nominative use — naming the project in order to refer to it truthfully — is
fine and needs no permission:

- "uses hushgate", "built on hushgate", "compatible with hushgate";
- "a fork of hushgate", "based on hushgate";
- writing about hushgate: articles, talks, benchmarks, comparisons, tutorials,
  including critical ones;
- keeping the name in file paths, imports, configuration keys and the
  `hushgate` command inside a fork you use privately or contribute back from.

## What you may not do

Anything that presents your thing as being the project, or as endorsed by it:

- publishing a fork, distribution, hosted service, appliance or commercial
  product **under the name** "hushgate", or under a name confusingly similar to
  it (hushgate.io, HushGate Pro, hushgate-cloud, Hushgateway, and so on);
- using the logo or banner as the mark of your own product, or in a way that
  suggests an official relationship;
- registering a domain, npm package, PyPI package, crates.io name, Docker Hub
  namespace, GitHub organisation, app-store listing or social media handle that
  presents itself as the official project or its official channel;
- using the name in advertising, a company name, or a product name in a way
  that implies sponsorship, affiliation or certification.

If you fork and publish, give it your own name. Saying "X, a fork of hushgate"
underneath your own name is exactly right.
